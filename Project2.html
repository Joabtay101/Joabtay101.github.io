Caerdydd Dragon

A 2D puzzle platformer set within Cardiff Castle, featuring the Welsh dragon as protagonist. Built in Unity (C#) with a team of four, with a focus on accessibility, cultural identity, and playtested mechanics.

Overview

Caerdydd Dragon is a 2D puzzle platformer inspired by the cultural heritage of Wales. The game features the iconic Welsh dragon navigating levels set within Cardiff Castle, collecting daffodils along the way. The project was a full team effort across all four members, covering game mechanics, story, and overall design — delivered within a fixed timeframe.

Project Goals

Gameplay

Deliver an intuitive yet challenging experience with accessible mechanics for a wide range of players.

Cultural Identity

Capture the essence of Welsh culture through art, themes, and level design rooted in real Cardiff landmarks.

Technical Execution

Implement polished platforming mechanics, responsive controls, and smooth camera handling.

Replayability

Encourage player engagement through collectibles, difficulty progression, and refined mechanics.

Key Features

  • Movement & Camera Smooth, accessible player movement with a camera system tested for intuitive feel across all skill levels.
  • Progressive Difficulty Two levels each introducing new mechanics — ensuring a natural learning curve for players.
  • Wind Zones A unique environmental mechanic adding challenge and variety to level traversal.
  • Enemy Interactions Jump-on enemy collision system that rewards skillful play.
  • Collectibles Daffodil collectibles spread across levels to encourage exploration and replayability.

Technical Specifications

  • Engine & Language Unity with C# scripting throughout.
  • Git Workflow Master branch for final submission, development branch for ongoing work, feature branches for isolated implementations.
  • Singleton Pattern Used for global state management across scenes.
  • UI Animation System Modular and reusable across multiple scenes.
  • Enemy AI Patrol and collision-response behaviours implemented per enemy type.

Playtesting & Feedback

20 participants playtested the game across both levels, providing structured feedback on accessibility, mechanics comprehension, and overall enjoyment.

Overall satisfaction score across key metrics

The Welsh cultural theme was well-received, with most players rating the overall experience 4–5 out of 5. The Wind Zone was noted as engaging but appropriately challenging — a deliberate design balance.

Evaluation & Conclusion

We successfully met our original objectives of accessibility, cultural representation, and engaging gameplay — and as a team we were proud of what we delivered within the time constraints. Playtesting confirmed the design held up with real players, and while we identified areas for future iteration, we considered the project a clear success.
